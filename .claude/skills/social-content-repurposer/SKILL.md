# Content repurposer

This skill turns one strong piece of content into a week of posts that each read as though they were written for their platform first. The thinking is already done in the source, so the work here is extraction and adaptation: finding the ideas that stand on their own, matching each to a format, and rewriting it in each platform's register without losing the author's voice.

## When another skill owns this

Some repurposing requests must go elsewhere, and you should hand them over rather than draft them here, because Fredrick's brand skills carry rules this general skill does not, such as verification, sign-offs and evidence grading. DataExplained repurposing goes to the DataExplained skills without exception. Fredrick's DataExplained social master skill runs LinkedIn and X as two independent editorial pipelines and forbids rewriting a LinkedIn topic into an X post or the reverse, which is the exact move this skill exists to make, and DataExplained reels and pins have their own skills too. When repurposing for Fredrick's personal brand, his LinkedIn derivative belongs to linkedin-artifact-engine if it rests on evidence or audit findings, so draft the other platforms here and pass that one over. Anything described as content engine or full pack goes to the skill built for that.

## Step 1: profile and inputs

The first step is reading the profiles in the social-context skill and working out whose voice the derivatives need. When the request does not make that clear, ask one short question, because nine drafts in the wrong voice are nine drafts to throw away. If you are working inside an EvoNexus workspace and `workspace/social/[C] social-context.md` exists, read it too and let it override the built-in profiles. Social-context notes that LinkedIn is the only platform confirmed for Fredrick's personal brand, so ask which accounts he runs before drafting for the others.

You then need the source, the target platforms (or permission to recommend them), any formats the user wants and a timeline. If the user pastes the source and names a platform, start work, since over-asking stalls a job that is mostly reading.

## Step 2: choose derivatives from the matrix

Each kind of source has derivatives that suit it best, and the matrix below is the starting point.

| Source | Best derivatives |
|---|---|
| Blog post or article | LinkedIn post (key insight), X thread (takeaways), carousel (framework or tips), Threads or Bluesky post (casual take), Pinterest pin (evergreen how-to), three to five standalone posts |
| Newsletter | Thread expanding the main argument, a single post carrying the one sentence everyone needs, a carousel for the framework |
| Podcast or video transcript | Quote posts from the best three lines, a short-video clip script for TikTok, Reels or Shorts, a thread of key moments, a carousel of insights |
| Existing social post | Adaptations for other platforms (longer for LinkedIn and Facebook, shorter for X, Threads and Bluesky), a thread, a short-video version |
| Case study or story | Thread with a narrative arc, LinkedIn long-form post, before-and-after carousel, short-video story |
| How-to or tutorial | Step-by-step carousel, one-step-per-post thread, tips spread across the week, Pinterest pin, YouTube Short |

Some formats simply do not exist on some platforms, so check the fit before promising a derivative. Threads in the multi-post sense do not exist on TikTok, YouTube or Pinterest, and carousels in this skill's sense belong to LinkedIn and Instagram. A short video needs someone to film or produce it, so confirm that before drafting one, and a Pinterest pin only makes sense when there is an image and a page to send people to.

## Step 3: extract and rank insights

Read the source and pull out three to seven standalone insights. An insight qualifies only if it makes sense without the rest of the source, because a derivative that needs the original to be understood is a referral rather than a post. For each one, capture the core claim in a sentence, the supporting example, figure or story beat, and who it is most valuable for.

```
Source: blog post, "Why we moved first-round interviews to async"
Insights extracted: 5

1. Time to hire dropped by [number]% after the switch (data insight)
2. The best hire recorded her interview at midnight (story insight)
3. Async removes time-zone bias from the first round (framework insight)
4. A three-step process for running your first async round (how-to insight)
5. Most objections to async are objections to change (contrarian insight)
```

Rank them from strongest to weakest standalone value by asking whether someone who saw only that post would find it worth their time; if not, reframe it or merge it with another. The top insight becomes the anchor derivative, and the rest become supporting posts across the week. Any figure in an insight must come from the source or the user, so carry placeholders through rather than filling them in.

## Step 4: match each insight to a format

The insight's shape decides its format, as the table shows.

| Insight type | Best format |
|---|---|
| Step-by-step process | Carousel or thread |
| Single counterintuitive claim | Standalone post or thread opener |
| Story with a lesson | Thread, LinkedIn long-form post or short-video story |
| Data point | Standalone post with context |
| Framework or model | Carousel, one slide per element |
| Quote or memorable line | Quote post, or the spoken hook of a short video |

When one derivative needs more depth, social-carousel-writer, social-thread-writer and social-post-writer hold the full craft for those formats.

## Step 5: draft each derivative natively

The voice stays the same across platforms while the register shifts, and every derivative is rewritten rather than transplanted. Rely only on the limits below. For anything the table leaves out, check the platform's current rules.

| Platform | Limits | Register and shape |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Up to 3,000 characters; 1,200 to 1,500 recommended for feed posts; first two or three lines show before "see more" | Thoughtful and story-forward, three to five short paragraphs, hook before the cut |
| X | 280 characters per post on a standard account; longer needs X Premium | Sharp and opinionated, direct opening, numbered posts for threads |
| Threads | 500 characters per post | Casual, like a message to a smart friend; shorter often reads better |
| Bluesky | 300 characters per post | Conversational and direct, one clear point per post |
| Instagram | Captions up to 2,200 characters, opening line or so before "more"; carousels up to 20 slides | Visual first, caption supports the image: hook, depth, action, hashtags last |
| Facebook | Long posts allowed; first few lines show before "See more" | Conversational, with the hook inside the visible lines |
| TikTok | First one to three seconds decide whether people keep watching; check the current caption limit | Spoken script, hook said aloud and shown as on-screen text |
| YouTube | Titles up to 100 characters, descriptions up to 5,000; Shorts up to three minutes | Shorts follow the TikTok pattern; long videos get a title and description derivative |
| Pinterest | Titles up to 100 characters, about 40 visible in the grid; descriptions under 800 characters, as Fredrick's Pinterest skill keeps them | Search-led and evergreen, with the key words at the front of the title |

The visible preview is tiny on almost every platform, which is why every derivative needs its own first line rather than the source's opening. Instagram Reels follow the same short-video pattern as TikTok and Shorts, so write each short video as a script in this shape:

```
Hook (spoken, first one to three seconds): [line]
On-screen text: [the hook in a few words]
Beats: [two to four points, one per cut]
Close (spoken): [one action]
Caption: [platform caption]
```

## Priority ranking and schedule

Once the drafts exist, rank them by likely reach against effort, with a one-line reason per item. The default below is a starting judgement rather than a measurement, so adjust it to the user's platform mix and to whatever social-performance-analyzer finds in their own data.

1. **X thread**, because it costs little to produce and has the most upside when the hook lands.
2. **LinkedIn post**, which suits a professional audience and leaves room for context.
3. **Carousel**, more effort, though people save carousels and come back to them.
4. **Threads or Bluesky post**, low effort and useful for testing an angle cheaply.
5. **Quote posts**, easy to batch, since one source usually yields three to five.
6. **Short video**, the highest effort, worth it only when someone can film or produce it.

The schedule should spread derivatives across five to seven days, open with the anchor derivative, and space the rest at least 24 hours apart, because posting everything on one day floods the feed and makes each post compete with the others. Deliver it as a plain schedule the user can follow:

```
Repurposing schedule, week of [date]

Day 1 (Monday): [platform], [derivative type]
[full post copy]

Day 2 (Tuesday): [platform], [derivative type]
[full post copy]
```

A plan that runs beyond one week belongs to social-content-calendar.

## What makes repurposing feel lazy

These mistakes make repurposed content read as spam, and each has a cheap fix.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Identical text on every platform | Followers on two platforms read the same post twice | Rewrite for each platform's register |
| Opening with "I wrote a blog post about" | That is a referral, not a post | Lead with the insight itself |
| All derivatives on the same day | Floods the feed and splits attention | Spread them across the week |
| Keeping the source's formatting | A blog's bullet list feels wrong on Threads or in a script | Reformat for the medium |
| Generic takeaways | "Great insights in this post" carries nothing | Pull the actual claim |
| Ignoring limits | A LinkedIn-length draft cannot run on X or Bluesky | Write to the limit from the start |

One insight written natively for three platforms shows the register shifting while the voice holds:

```
LinkedIn:
We moved first-round interviews to recorded video [time period] ago, and time to hire dropped by [number]%. The candidate who convinced us recorded her answers at midnight, because that was the only hour her current job left free. A live slot at 2pm would have filtered her out before we ever saw how good she was.

X:
A 2pm interview slot would have filtered out the best hire I've made. She recorded her answers at midnight because that was her only free hour. Async first rounds find the people your calendar hides.

Threads:
The best hire I've made did her first interview as a recording at midnight, since that was the only free hour she had. A 2pm slot would have lost her.
```

## Output format and final check

Deliver the work in four parts: a one-sentence summary of the source and its core argument; the derivatives, grouped by platform, each with its format, the full draft and any platform note; the priority ranking; and the schedule. Put it all in your reply, and only when working inside an EvoNexus workspace also save it under `workspace/social/`.

Before handing anything over, load the `house-prose-style` skill and run its pre-delivery voice check on every derivative, then fix what fails. Fredrick's standing rule is that no copy goes out unless it follows the house style, and repurposed drafts need the check more than most, because adapting the same idea nine times is exactly how a single tic gets copied into every post. Check character counts against the platform table at the same time, and confirm every figure traces to the source or the user.

## Boundaries

This skill adapts existing content, so an original post written from scratch belongs to social-post-writer, and choosing pillars or a content strategy belongs to social-content-strategy. Deciding which platforms deserve the effort is social-platform-strategy's job, while measuring how the derivatives performed belongs to social-performance-analyzer. The output is text, including video scripts, rather than images, footage or design files.
